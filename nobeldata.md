# Nobel Prize Guessing-Game Data Pack: All Six Prizes, 2006–2025

Every one of the six prizes was awarded in every year from 2006 to 2025, so there are no empty years. The only irregularity was Literature 2018: the Swedish Academy scandal delayed it, and it was announced together with the 2019 prize on 10 October 2019.

## TL;DR
- **Number of laureates per prize:**
  - Physics: 3 laureates in 13 of 20 years, and never a solo winner.
  - Chemistry: 3 laureates in 14 of 20 years.
  - Medicine: 3 laureates in 11 of 20 years.
  - Economics: 6 solo years, 6 two-way years and 8 three-way years.
  - Literature: always one laureate.
  - Peace: one laureate in 13 of 20 years. Six years went to organisations alone.
- **Women:** 34 female laureates across all six prizes (counted by prize year). That is 0–5 per year, with a median of 1 and a peak of 5 in 2009. Five years had none: 2006, 2010, 2012, 2016 and 2017.
- **Africa and citation wording:**
  - Laureates living in Africa when they won: Sirleaf (Liberia), Gbowee (Ghana), the Tunisian Dialogue Quartet, Mukwege (DR Congo) and Abiy Ahmed (Ethiopia). All are Peace laureates. Le Clézio (Literature 2008) is a partial case: nobelprize.org gives his residence as "France; Mauritius".
  - Physics citations most often start "for the discovery of…". Chemistry citations most often start "for the development of…". Medicine citations are dominated by "for their discoveries…", and some form of "discover-" appears in 19 of 20 years.

## How to read the tables
- **Sources:** nobelprize.org prize lists and press releases are the main source. Citations are quoted from them, and where laureates in one year had different citations, each is shown. Laureate details were cross-checked against Wikipedia's per-prize lists.
- **Affiliation** is the institution at the time of the award. **Residence** for science and economics laureates is inferred from the affiliation's country.
- **Citizenship:** nobelprize.org doesn't publish citizenship for science laureates; its country labels show where the laureate was affiliated. The citizenship column therefore comes from biographical sources. "US" includes dual citizens. "?" means sources conflict or I couldn't check.
- **Age** is the age at the October announcement. "~" means only the birth year is known, so the age may be off by one.
- **Aggregator warning:** third-party lists contain errors. For example, the areppim list gives Leonid Hurwicz's citizenship as "Russia" (he was a US citizen). Check any disputed cell against nobelprize.org.

- "for experiments / experimental methods…": 2022, 2023.
